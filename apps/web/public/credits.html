Seeded math functions

The library contains 13 equations transcribed from the user-supplied @indent126 reel and the ten named functions from the @the.lab67 reel. Five distinct functions are selected per coin and unlock at $100K, $500K, $1M, $2M and $5M. They play finite, tempo-synchronized phrases in the existing vanilla Pure Data voices. Cartesian plots progressively draw the literal equations at the same phrase position as the bounded audio controls. The first reel supplies equations without names, so those titles are descriptive. Data microtones

The new set uses original vanilla Pure Data sources: five finite seeded voices—high ticks, a longer sine pulse, filtered noise, middle pulses and a low sine cluster. Rhythm follows the market tempo and measured activity; movement shapes pitch, timbre and filtering; observed buy/sell balance shapes stereo position. Synthesis principles were studied in Sound Simulator’s tutorial and its creator-provided patch. Its source or recordings are not bundled as an Ikeda work. This is an authored market adaptation.

EarthBound instruments and melodic patterns

The active melodic engine uses 15 presets extracted from the project owner's EarthBound.sf2. The coin seed selects a stable instrument. Preset provenance, source checksum and sample metadata. These samples are user-supplied; the CC0 license of the previous piano pack does not apply to them. Extracted mono samples retain their source loop points and tuning, with measured level trims and instrument-specific envelopes, note ranges, filtering and room sends. Market movements of at least 5% select notes; market cap and contextual activity shape finite interlocking passages. The patterns are authored for UPIC, informed by additive composition and analysis of a supplied MIDI, rather than playback of the reference recording. Future notes follow the current harmony and tonic. Tempo changes land at pattern bar boundaries. Falling activity softens the passage and can end it at the next boundary. The shared reverb and six-voice budget bound audio workload. Pause and seek clear queued notes and the room.

Connected decoded feeds can allow sparse notes after 30 seconds without an observed trade, with subsequent coin-seeded 45–60-second spacing. Active live trade phrases can retrigger every four beats; ≥5% price moves advance the harmony, and seeded arpeggios can join eligible phrase windows with a 12-beat cooldown. Replay applies movement selection to completed candles and allows quiet notes during zero-volume candles; quiet spacing uses listening time. No reference-video audio or CoDiCodec model is bundled.

Open source musical AI

Exchange feeds

CCXT 4.5.85, MIT licensed, supplies public Kraken, Coinbase Exchange and Binance spot markets. The official browser build is loaded on demand in a worker. License · Provenance. Received trades drive chart, music and visuals together. Order-book depth covers at most 25 levels within 1% of midpoint and is not pool liquidity. React Bits interface components

CometDial, LatticeLoader, Counter and the PixelBlast shader by David Haz / React Bits. PixelBlast retains the original evolving noise and ripple construction. Its numerical appearance controls follow chart data and measured sound: square size, pattern scale and density, speed, size variation, edge taper, ripple controls, liquid controls and grain. An additional regional noise layer distributes activity across the field; central coin-image formation is disabled. Liquid displacement changes field samples before crisp square rasterization. The colour stays monochrome, and a fixed grid lets squares grow in place. Quiet intervals leave smaller, weaker pixels. WebGL and Canvas share geometry and a bounded render resolution. Replay and liquid motion use integrated clocks that preserve phase on pause. Live parameter values are available in the pdata inspector. Source comparison and parameter mappings. Counter is adapted for the market clock, rollover, reduced motion and replay seeking. Used with React and Motion inside this application.
