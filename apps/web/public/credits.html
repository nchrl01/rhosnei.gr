Market phrasing

The current phrase composer uses 301 pooled pitch, rhythm, articulation and activity distributions from 31,136 usable unique arrangements in the VGMusic archive. Generated two-bar motifs recur through an eight-bar introduce, answer, vary and return form, alongside market-driven chip packets. The model contains aggregate lead, bass and drum statistics, not retrieved songs or a neural generator; harmony and phrase form are authored by UPIC. Malformed source files and the unavailable new-submissions page are excluded.

The shared sound driver screens final learned lead pitches against nine-note source interval runs, changing or withholding matches. Browser loading is asynchronous, so the chip’s support and packets can play while learned lead awaits verification; offline rendering prepares the same index. This reduces indexed reuse without guaranteeing that music has no perceived resemblance. The earlier contour library retains 13 equations transcribed from the user-supplied @indent126 reel and ten named functions from the @the.lab67 reel, adapted into bounded musical contours.

Game Boy transmission instrument

All current sound is generated by two MAME-derived Game Boy APUs. Each has two pulse channels, one programmable 32-step, four-bit wave channel and one clocked noise channel. The foreground chip carries lead, arpeggios, bass and drums; the harmony chip carries held chord tones, a wave drone and texture. The browser and offline artwork renderer share the same eight-slot rack, register compiler and sound core. There is no parallel sample instrument, Kraken bass player, granular PCM engine or conventional reverb bus. The earlier original APU core remains inactive.

The drone’s resonance comes from harmonic emphasis in its four-bit waveform, not a claimed Game Boy resonant filter. Quieter arpeggio echoes are scored repeats that consume the same pulse lane. Lead and arp response windows, chord openings and later drone/texture entries share an eight-bar form and common rests; harmony changes every two bars.

The JavaScript sound host is adapted from MAME’s Game Boy sound device, credited to Wilbert Pol and Anthony Kruize, with thanks to Shay Green, under BSD-3-Clause. UPIC adapts its device state to browser and offline audio and applies documented Pan Docs corrections. License · Source provenance and adaptation notes. The market-to-register composer remains UPIC’s own implementation.

Tero Parviainen’s Stochastic Drum Machine informed probability-based variation. UPIC uses its own coin-specific prime periods and phase offsets, with market fingerprints controlling gates, observed volume controlling bursts, and motion controlling noise clocks. These asynchronous packets have no fixed kick/backbeat template; they leave room for the generated motif and share its rests. The reference’s samples and runtime are not bundled.

Pan Docs supplies the hardware reference for pulse-phase retention, CH3 sample buffering, extreme noise clocks, stereo volume and DAC/DC-removal corrections. The reviewed GB303 playback source demonstrates disabling CH3, loading 16 wave-RAM bytes, and re-enabling it; no GB303 code or wave assets were copied. Ken Shirriff’s amplifier analysis concerns the separate speaker/headphone amplification stage, not the APU generators.

The host retains chip counters, envelopes, noise state and stereo routing. Normal rests keep DACs connected to avoid unnecessary switching at every note ending. The implementation is sound-only: it does not execute a CPU or ROM, reproduce MAME’s entire output pipeline, or claim exact physical-hardware accuracy. MAME itself marks Game Boy sound imperfect; console-revision quirks and the physical speaker/headphone amplifier remain limitations. The former rendered libraries remain credited as archives: GB library metadata · Original waveform provenance · Archived Kraken sample provenance.

Fresh observations authorize finite sound windows. Missing, stale or zero activity stops new excitation; pause and seek cancel queued events. Frozen observations are interpreted by the same composer and chip path, without claiming to recover an unrecorded live performance.

Exchange feeds

CCXT 4.5.85, MIT licensed, supplies public Kraken, Coinbase Exchange and Binance spot markets. The official browser build is loaded on demand in a worker. License · Provenance. Received trades drive chart, music and visuals together. Order-book depth covers at most 25 levels within 1% of midpoint and is not pool liquidity. Coinbase depth uses public five-second snapshots; its trade feed uses WebSocket. Historical quote volume is estimated from candle base volume times close. USDT/USDC pairs use quote prices as a labelled USD proxy. Supported native currencies use centrally cached address-balance samples and indexed market-cap estimates. Sample sizes are not total holder counts; exchange assets without a supported native adapter have no native balance feed. Availability depends on each venue, browser permissions and region.

Interface references

$UPIC’s header mark is the user-supplied image, dithered with the same pixel engine. The live/transport lamp follows the compact dot treatment on NTS, with explicit live, delayed, historical and paused states.

Full-image ordered Bayer dithering inspired by Tooooools. Coin thumbnails share one image-level-aware 8×8 Bayer pixel treatment. Restricted CDN images use the artwork relay; unavailable artwork uses a placeholder. The current Earthbound visual uses the October 7 Canvas pattern-count renderer, with coin artwork, market cap, liquidity and available holder observations shaping the field. Price direction changes mark shapes; observed activity accelerates the loop, while quiet intervals retain a gentle drift. Accepted chip events add small, timed luminance accents without changing holder geometry. This mapping does not measure audio amplitude. Candle replays use a reconstructed population rather than historical holder records. The earlier horizontal-row renderer remains an inactive source.

The light floating-label field and price-history tick navigator are independent implementations inspired by Bencho’s label input and progress ticks. The tick readouts and heights use UPIC’s loaded market data.

Native balance sources

Native address samples are cached in Cloudflare for one to two hours and expire after six hours. Providers include SoChain, Blockscout, Solana Vibe Station, Ankr XRP, Alchemy, Bitcore, TronGrid, TzKT, Hedera, dRPC, Chainz, Koios, Cosmos Directory, Kaspa, Toncenter, FASTNEAR, Stellar Horizon, Hiro, Aptos Labs, Sui public GraphQL, VeChain Thorest, and official chain explorers and public RPCs. Powered by Routescan.io APIs. Sample coverage varies: recent participants, a ledger page, or a richest-address list. Balances can belong to contracts, custodians or exchanges; they do not count people. Shielded balances and other chain-specific omissions are not inferred.

Native price and market-cap reference data are powered by CoinGecko, with CoinLore as an independent free fallback, using exact coin IDs in shared hourly batches. Current exchange prices scale the latest indexed circulating-supply estimate. Provider timestamps are retained when supplied; otherwise retrieval time is kept separately. Preview images and recorded visual frames are stored centrally for catalogue assets.
