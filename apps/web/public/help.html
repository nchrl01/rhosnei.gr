How it works

UPIC turns market activity into an evolving field of sound and image.

Start listening

  1. Search for a coin name, contract address or pair such as BTC/USD. You can also choose a preview in the Marketplace.
  2. Selecting a search result starts playback. Use Play or Pause to control the experience; pausing lets the visual slow and fade. Adjust listening volume in the header.
  3. Use the arrow on the visual to fill the screen. Use it again, or press Escape, to return.

Read the visual

Holders
More reported holders create a denser field, up to 10,000 marks. If a total is unavailable, the available holder sample is used.
Liquidity
Higher liquidity makes marks larger along the coin image. The image guides their sizes.
Market cap
Shapes how widely the field spreads. At $50 million, colours from the coin artwork appear.
Movement
The market sets the pace. Upward changes can turn marks into +; downward changes into ×. Some + marks glow green and some × marks red. Dots keep the visual's own palette.

Explore its history

Choose Replay and select a moment on the chart or timeline. Use 1×, 50× or 100× to set the pace. Choose Live to return to the latest available market data.

Drag the chart to explore, and scroll or pinch to zoom. The timeline also works with the arrow keys.

Replay uses saved visual frames where available. Elsewhere, it creates a visual from the available chart history. History can differ between networks and trading pairs.

A closer look

Use the bookmark beside Play to save a coin for later. Sign in with your wallet, then find your coins under Profile → Saved on any device using the same account.

Open Advanced view for market readings, holder counts, liquidity and data-source details. The chart stays available in the main player.

For coins on several networks, the Marketplace opens a supported market using available liquidity and artwork, with established networks preferred when capacity is similar.

Collect the artwork

The collection section sits below Advanced view. It shows a coin’s collection and the indexed owner of its first edition when that information is available.

A collection can have several editions. Claiming an available edition means becoming its owner; it does not give you ownership of every edition or of the underlying cryptocurrency.

Tokenization and edition claims are not available on the live site yet.
