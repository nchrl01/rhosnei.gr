Data microtones and holder drone

The new set uses original vanilla Pure Data sources: five seeded voices—high ticks, sustained sine, filtered noise, middle pulses and a low sine cluster—plus a holder-count drone. Rhythm follows the market tempo and measured activity; movement shapes pitch, timbre and filtering; observed buy/sell balance shapes stereo position. Synthesis principles were studied in Sound Simulator’s tutorial and its creator-provided patch. Its source or recordings are not bundled as an Ikeda work. This is an authored market adaptation.

Free GeckoTerminal token metadata supplies holder-count snapshots where available. Logarithmic wallet count controls drone fullness and brightness; the top ten wallets’ supply share controls beating. Holders include exchanges, contracts and treasuries; they are not a listener count. Metadata is requested every two minutes through the shared provider queue. Snapshot age is displayed; influence remains full through one hour, fades to zero by six hours and stays off for missing or unknown timestamps. This expiry is an app policy, not a provider freshness guarantee. Candle history has no historical holders, so its holder drone stays off. Current watchers are unavailable.

Trade piano and arpeggios

The trade piano uses CC0 VSCO 2 CE upright-piano recordings by Simon Dalzell / Ivy Audio, distributed by Versilian Studios. License · Sample provenance. Piano plays individual harmonic notes at 5% cumulative price movement since its previous note. An initial contextual price anchors the first move. The light sample pack keeps eight notes in 16 kHz mono PCM16, trimmed to six seconds and normalized offline. Fewer overlapping voices, complete release ramps and a fixed stereo room preserve bounded playback workload. The piano retains its clear sampled attack with longer releases and a stronger, memory-capped eight-second diffuse reverb; cap controls warmth and the wet/dry balance. Harmonic stages advance with accepted notes, with nearest-tone movement and deterministic timing/touch variations. Connected decoded feeds can allow quiet single notes after 30 seconds without an observed trade, then at coin-seeded 45–60-second intervals. Replay applies movement selection to completed candles and allows quiet notes only during zero-volume candles; its quiet spacing uses listening time. Arpeggio phrases are eligible only after significant moves. Harmonic strings, Gameta, ZERO100 and Perc Generator are removed from the active engine. The standalone hardstyle instrument is removed from the active engine. No video audio or CoDiCodec model is bundled in the website.

Open source musical AI and voice

Occasional arpeggios use Magenta MusicRNN chord-pitches improv. Neural contour is generated once per coin using neural probabilities and a coin-seeded draw, then projected onto the current piano chord. A seeded phrase plays while the model loads or when it is unavailable. Shared scores freeze that phrase. Magenta license · TensorFlow license.

Phrasing references: Tone.js humanization and Magenta expressive timing and dynamics. These inform authored phrasing; no Performance RNN model or Tone.js runtime is loaded. Coin names use Kokoro 82M, quantized ONNX, female voice af_nicole at 0.78 speaking speed, followed by an authored noise-vocoder whisper effect that reduces pitched excitation and preserves a little articulation. This is a processed voice, not a whisper-trained model. Inference runs in a worker on the listener’s device; model and voice files download from Hugging Face and the matching ONNX runtime from jsDelivr. Voice passes through a warm 4.8-second stereo hall with 32 ms predelay and a filtered reverb return. Announcements require playback, measured market movement and audible output from the other instruments. A dedicated instrument tap excludes the voice and its own reverb. Announcements stop when that instrument signal becomes silent, have a bounded duration, and share the master volume and audio capture. No browser speech voice is substituted. Interface references

$UPIC’s header mark is the user-supplied image, dithered with the same pixel engine. The live/transport lamp follows the compact dot treatment on NTS, with explicit live, delayed, historical and paused states.

Full-image error-diffusion dithering inspired by Tooooools. Readable image pixels use fine diffusion; hosts that block pixel access use a source-only SVG stochastic dither. There is no edge fade. The responsive signal field is an original canvas rendering of market measurements and the measured audible spectrum, informed by Ryoji Ikeda’s Datamatics and the complementary barcode bands in Sound Simulator’s Pure Data tutorial. It includes a dimensional price-history field, scanning barcode bands and measured audio spectrum, with movement tied to playback and real market activity.

The light floating-label field and price-history tick navigator are independent implementations inspired by Bencho’s label input and progress ticks. The tick readouts and heights use AV’s loaded market data.
